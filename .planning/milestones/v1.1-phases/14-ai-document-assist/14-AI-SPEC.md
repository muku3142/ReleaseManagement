## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Content Generation (document transform: clean up, summarize, custom instruction; no retrieval)

**Description:**
A release manager opens a generated release note, runbook or approval document and runs Clean up, Summarize or a custom instruction. ReleaseManagement sends only the document Markdown plus an instruction (from `app.assistant.*` config, or typed by the user) to rag-service `POST /v1/transform`, relays the token stream to the browser, and shows it beside the original. The user can edit the result as Markdown. Clean up and custom results can be saved as a new AI-assisted revision with the instruction recorded; summaries are view, copy or download only; the original revision is never altered. There is no retrieval and no ticket or commit context (D-14-08). "Good" means the same document, tidier: every ticket key, version, date, environment name and step order is preserved, nothing is invented, Markdown structure survives, and the user sees any difference before saving.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **Facts changed or invented.** A ticket key, version, date, environment or runbook step is dropped, altered, reordered or added, and the result is saved and exported to approvers. Mitigations: the instruction text, the ticket-key and version diff before Save (D-14-11), the verify banner (D-14-12), and an unaltered original.
2. **Truncated output saved as a full document.** `finishReason=length`, `done.truncated`, `error{DEADLINE}` or a cut-off prompt. A saved revision becomes current in exports and the assistant index (D-14-01), so Save must be blocked on any of these.
3. **A summary or unreviewed AI result replaces the real document.** Revisions are append-only (D-05-06), badged AI-assisted, and a summary saved under D-14-13 can be undone by regenerating.
4. **Prompt injection through document or instruction text.** Golden T03 fails 0/3 on the dev model (accepted for dev only; the production model must pass T03 before v1.1 ships). Defences are rag-service fencing and the human review step; nothing is applied automatically.
5. **Document content leaks into logs or storage.** Never log document, instruction or result text (SAFE-03). Discard persists nothing.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Software release management and IT change management (ITIL-style change and approval records, deployment runbooks, stakeholder release notes). Internal tooling at an enterprise software team.

**User Population:** The release manager (author and sole user of the AI feature). Downstream readers of the saved result: change managers and approvers (a CAB or equivalent) who read the approval document, on-call or deployment engineers who execute the runbook (often at night, under pressure), and business stakeholders who read release notes.

**Stakes Level:** High for runbooks and approval documents; Medium for release notes. (Not Critical: a human reviews the result beside the original and nothing is applied automatically.)

**Output Consequence:** A saved AI-assisted revision becomes the current revision, so it is what gets exported, shown to approvers and followed during a deployment. A dropped or reordered runbook step, a missing rollback trigger or a softened risk statement can cause an outage, a failed rollback or an approval granted on a misleading picture. The revision is immutable (DOC-07), so a bad one cannot be edited away; it can only be superseded, and it stays in the audit trail.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

```
Dimension: Step integrity and order (runbook)
Good (domain expert would accept): Every deploy step, post-deploy validation step and rollback step in the original is present once, in the same order, with the same commands, paths, flags, environment names and owners. Numbering may be tidied but the sequence is identical.
Bad (domain expert would flag): A step is merged into another, dropped, reordered, split so a command spans two steps, or reworded so a command or value changes. Any step added that was not in the original.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Runbook practice: executed top to bottom under pressure, with rollback defined before the first irreversible step and the point of no return called out (deployment runbook guidance, enov8; rollback checklists). Order carries meaning.
```

```
Dimension: Rollback triggers and backout plan preserved
Good: Each rollback trigger condition keeps its exact threshold and decision owner ("error rate above 2% for 5 minutes, release manager decides"). The backout plan, the rollback target (version, image tag) and any "point of no return" or irreversible-migration warning survive unchanged.
Bad: A threshold, duration or owner is dropped or generalised ("if errors rise"). The point-of-no-return warning is removed or moved after the step it protects. A rollback section is shortened to a summary.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Rollback guidance (triggers as metric thresholds or explicit decision criteria; named decide/execute/verify owners); DOC-03 requires rollback with explicit trigger conditions.
```

```
Dimension: Risk, impact and backout wording fidelity (approval/change document)
Good: Risk level, impact scope, affected systems, downtime window and approval conditions keep their strength and certainty. "Will cause up to 15 minutes of downtime" stays definite; "may" stays "may"; "must not" stays a prohibition. Any tidying leaves the meaning identical.
Bad: Risk is softened ("high" to "moderate", "will" to "may", a caveat or condition removed), strengthened without basis, or a negation flips ("not required" to "required"). A downtime window or affected-system list is shortened.
Stakes: Critical
Source: ITIL change records and CAB practice: approvers decide on the stated risk, impact and backout plan; DOC-04 requires labelled risk, impact and backout-plan sections. Unfaithful rewriting of fluent text is a recognised LLM risk (summarization faithfulness research, arXiv 2510.09915; arXiv 2507.11508).
```

```
Dimension: Identifier and fact preservation
Good: Every ticket key (PROJ-123), version, build number, date and time (with timezone), environment name (DEV, TEST, UAT, PROD) and hostname in the original appears in the result, spelled identically, attached to the same item.
Bad: A key is dropped, mistyped, or invented. An environment is swapped or lost. A version or date is changed or "normalised". A key moves to a different item (right key, wrong ticket description).
Stakes: Critical
Source: Practitioner standard: release notes and change records are traceability artefacts; auditors and approvers trace each change back to a ticket and environment. Invented or misattached identifiers defeat that trace.
```

```
Dimension: Structure and audience fit
Good: Headings, section labels (Deploy, Post-deploy validation, Rollback; Risk, Impact, Backout), lists, tables and code blocks survive. Clean up fixes spelling, grammar and duplicated wording and keeps the document usable by its reader. A summary is clearly a summary and leaves out nothing a reader would need to act on, or is used for reading only.
Bad: Required sections renamed or merged, tables flattened to prose, code blocks reformatted so commands break, conversational preamble ("Here is the cleaned version") left in the saved text, a summary treated as a complete document.
Stakes: High (Medium for release notes)
Source: Template-defined section structure (DOC-03, DOC-04); the plain-text pitfall noted in Section 3. Practitioner expectation that a runbook can be followed without interpretation.
```

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Silent step loss during tidy-up.** The model "merges" or "condenses" adjacent steps, or drops a step it judges redundant (for example a verification or a manual approval pause). The result reads cleaner and looks complete, so the loss is hard to see side by side. Omission is a distinct and harder-to-spot error than fabrication (arXiv 2507.11508 and related omission work).
2. **Softened or strengthened risk language.** Rewriting for "professional tone" turns definite statements into hedges, or the reverse, and can reverse a negation. The approver then decides on a different risk than the author stated.
3. **Plausible but wrong identifiers.** A small model completes a pattern: invents a ticket key in sequence, "corrects" a version to a neighbour, or swaps an environment. The regex diff in D-14-11 catches added and missing keys and versions but not a key attached to the wrong item, nor environment names or dates.
4. **Truncation that reads as complete.** A near-limit document ends mid-step. The rollback section is usually last, so it is the part most likely to be cut, and a runbook with a missing or half rollback section is the worst case. Covered by the finishReason and truncated Save block.
5. **Summary or custom instruction mistaken for the record.** A summary or a custom rewrite ("make this shorter for executives") is saved and later exported to approvers or followed by an engineer as the runbook.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No regulation applies directly to the AI feature itself for this internal deployment (the model is local, so no data leaves the host). Indirect audit context applies and is best treated as a requirement on the revision record, not on the model:

- **Change control as audit evidence (SOX ITGC-style; also ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.8.32 and PCI DSS change requirements, if the organisation is in scope).** Auditors test change management as a control and ask for the population of production changes with requester, approver, timestamps, justification and test or deployment evidence. Practitioner sources describe segregation of duties (the author of a change is not its sole approver) and complete, tamper-evident records. The sources reviewed are vendor and practitioner writing, not the SOX or PCAOB text, so confirm specifics with the organisation's auditor.
- **Implication for this phase.** Immutable, append-only revisions (DOC-07, D-05-06) fit the evidence need. The AI-assisted flag, the recorded instruction and the source revision let an auditor see that a document was machine-edited and at whose request. The original must stay retrievable unchanged. An AI-assisted revision must not be presented as approved content: approval is a human act on a revision the approver has read.
- **Not identified:** HIPAA, FCA, and AI-specific regulation for a local, human-reviewed internal drafting aid. GDPR: no new personal data is introduced beyond what Jira ticket text may already contain; confirm whether ticket text can include customer or personal data.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** None (no AI framework). ReleaseManagement calls rag-service `POST /v1/transform` (Phase 10, RAG-11) through its existing Phase 12 `RestClient` adapter pattern behind a port; rag-service owns the Ollama call and fences the document as data (D-10-09). Selection carried over from the milestone research verdict and 10-AI-SPEC §2, not re-run.

**Version:** Spring Framework 7 `RestClient` (Spring Boot 4.1-managed); rag-service `/v1` contract in `rag-service/openapi.json`; dev model qwen2.5-coder:7b on Ollama 0.34.0, num_ctx 4096.

**Rationale:**
A single stateless call (text + instruction in, streamed text out) needs no orchestration, memory, retrieval or agents. rag-service is the project-wide AI boundary (domain-free, reusable), so ReleaseManagement only owns the instruction text (config, D-14-07) and the revision workflow.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Spring AI | Adds an abstraction over a one-call integration and would bypass rag-service, breaking the single-AI-boundary architecture (rag-service owns Ollama and the injection fences) |
| LangChain4j | Same: framework weight for one HTTP call; the milestone research verdict rejected AI frameworks |
| Calling Ollama directly from ReleaseManagement | Duplicates rag-service prompt fencing, limits and metadata-only logging (SAFE-01..03) |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — the model is config only (`RAG_CHAT_MODEL`); swapping providers is a rag-service adapter concern.

### Entry Point Pattern
Port, `RestClient` adapter with an SSE read loop, and an `SseEmitter` relay (Phase 12 adapter shape, Phase 13 browser streaming shape). Verify the `RestClient.exchange` usage and whether `BrowserListener`/`SseSink` from Phase 13 can be reused before copying.
```java
// ports/RagTransformPort.java
public interface RagTransformPort {
    TransformOutcome transform(String text, String instruction, TransformListener listener); // errors returned, never thrown
    interface TransformListener { void token(String text); void heartbeat(); }
}
public sealed interface TransformOutcome {
    record Done(String finishReason, boolean truncated, Integer promptTokens, Integer completionTokens) implements TransformOutcome {}
    record Failed(Reason reason, Integer maxChars, Integer retryAfterSeconds) implements TransformOutcome {}
    enum Reason { OFFLINE, BUSY, MODEL_MISSING, TOO_LARGE, DEADLINE, FAILED }
}

// adapters/rag/RagTransformAdapter.java
public TransformOutcome transform(String text, String instruction, TransformListener l) {
    try {
        return streamClient.post().uri("/v1/transform")
            .contentType(MediaType.APPLICATION_JSON).accept(MediaType.TEXT_EVENT_STREAM)
            .body(new TransformWire(text, instruction))
            .exchange((req, res) -> {
                if (res.getStatusCode().isError()) return mapProblem(res); // 413 TOO_LARGE, 429 BUSY, 503 OFFLINE/MODEL_MISSING
                return readSse(res.getBody(), l);
            });
    } catch (ResourceAccessException e) { return new TransformOutcome.Failed(Reason.OFFLINE, null, null); }
}
// readSse: BufferedReader.readLine loop, about 40 lines (same parser as the chat adapter).
//   ":" line -> l.heartbeat(); "event:"/"data:" until a blank line dispatches:
//   token -> l.token(text); done -> Done; error -> Failed(reason).
//   Stream ends with no done/error -> Failed(FAILED).

// adapters/web/AssistantTransformController.java
@PostMapping("/api/assistant/transform")   // no produces=, so 400/404/413 can still be problem+json
public ResponseEntity<SseEmitter> transform(@Valid @RequestBody TransformRequest req) {
    var doc = documents.resolve(req.releaseId(), req.documentType(), req.revision()); // 404 before the stream opens
    service.assertFits(doc.markdown(), instructionFor(req));                          // pre-stream 413, same message as the UI
    SseEmitter emitter = new SseEmitter(deadline.plusSeconds(30).toMillis());
    Thread.ofVirtual().name("assistant-transform").start(() -> service.run(doc.markdown(), instructionFor(req), emitter));
    return ResponseEntity.ok().contentType(MediaType.TEXT_EVENT_STREAM)
        .header("Cache-Control", "no-cache").header("X-Accel-Buffering", "no").body(emitter);
}
```
Browser events: `status`, `token`, `done{finishReason, truncated}`, `error{outcome}`, plus `: hb` comments, reusing Phase 13's outcome vocabulary.

### Common Pitfalls
1. **`TRANSFORM_SYSTEM` says "Output plain text" and never mentions Markdown.** Clean up can flatten headings, lists and tables. Every per-mode instruction must say to keep the Markdown structure, and the golden fixture should check it.
2. **The 413 limit depends on instruction length and the 256 slack, and assumes output about as long as input** (roughly half of `num_ctx`). A client estimate that ignores these lets requests reach a 413. Treat the server 413 as the authority and show the same message.
3. **Output can hit `num_predict` before the end.** `numPredict = numCtx - promptTokens`, so a near-limit document returns `finishReason=length` that looks like a normal `done`. Block Save and warn unless `finishReason` is `stop` and `truncated` is false.
4. **The JDK client's request timeout bounds time to headers, not the body** (A5 in the Phase 12 research, still unconfirmed). Use a separate stream client plus a total deadline in the listener, with the emitter timeout at deadline plus a margin.
5. **A client disconnect is noticed only on a write.** Forward rag heartbeats as `: hb` and close the upstream response on browser abort, otherwise rag-service's single in-flight slot stays held and the next request gets 429 until the 120 s deadline.
6. **Never send the document in a URL or log it.** Keep `toString` redaction on the request records.
7. **Jackson 3 package split:** `ObjectMapper` is `tools.jackson.databind`; annotations stay in `com.fasterxml.jackson.annotation`.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
The model is rag-service's concern (`RAG_CHAT_MODEL`); ReleaseManagement never sends a model, temperature or max tokens. Current rag-service values: `qwen2.5-coder:7b`, `num_ctx` 4096, transform temperature 0.3, `numPredict = min(4096, numCtx - promptEstimate)`, deadline 120 s, `PROMPT_VERSION` `p10-v2`. The production model is undecided; it must pass golden T03, and a bigger `num_ctx` raises the document limit through config only.

**Core Pattern:**
```
POST /api/assistant/transform {releaseId, documentType, revision, mode, instruction?}
  validate (400); resolve the stored revision (404)
  instruction = mode==CUSTOM ? request.instruction : props.prompts[mode][documentType]
  assertFits(markdown, instruction) -> 413 problem+json with maxChars
  SseEmitter; virtual thread: port.transform(...) relays tokens unchanged; done{finishReason,truncated} | error{outcome}
POST /api/assistant/documents/ai-revisions {releaseId, documentType, sourceRevision, mode, instruction, markdown}
  mode must be CLEAN_UP or CUSTOM (SUMMARIZE gives 400)
  append snapshot N+1 with copied template fields, ai_assisted=true, instruction recorded
```
The transform input is always the stored revision server-side, never client text, so the original cannot be altered. Edited Markdown is validated for size and NUL and stored as Markdown, never HTML (D-05-05). Save never calls the model.

**Tool Use:** None. A single stateless call; no `tools` field.

**State Management:** Server-side stateless; discard stores nothing. The only persisted state is the append-only saved revision (`ai_assisted`, `ai_instruction`, `ai_mode`). The edit buffer, stream state and the last 5 custom instructions live in browser memory only (D-14-09, D-13-12). Regenerate appends on top (D-14-02).

**Context Window Strategy:**
No chunking (D-14-10); the decision is made before sending. Client estimate, mirroring rag-service:
`limitChars = floor(((numCtx - systemTokens - ceil(len(instruction)/3.0) - 256) / 2) * 3.0)`
At 4096 with a 300-char instruction this is about 5,300 characters (my estimate, not measured). Serve it from a config-backed limits endpoint so the UI disables Clean up and Summarize and shows "This document is too long for the AI model on this server (about N of M characters)". The server re-checks with the same formula, and a rag-service 413 shows the same message. A bigger production model raises the limit through the `num-ctx` config.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

Substitution: this is a Java 25 / Spring Boot project, so records with Jakarta Bean Validation and Jackson 3 play the Pydantic role. Model output is plain text by design, so there is no output schema to validate and no validation retry. Structure sits at the edges: the request DTOs, the saved revision fields and the SSE payloads.

```java
public enum TransformMode { CLEAN_UP, SUMMARIZE, CUSTOM }
public enum DocumentType { RELEASE_NOTES, RUNBOOK, APPROVAL }

public record TransformRequest(
        @NotNull UUID releaseId, @NotNull DocumentType documentType, @Positive int revision,
        @NotNull TransformMode mode,
        @Size(max = 2000) String instruction) {}   // matches rag instruction-max-chars; required when mode==CUSTOM (service-checked)

public record SaveAiRevisionRequest(
        @NotNull UUID releaseId, @NotNull DocumentType documentType, @Positive int sourceRevision,
        @NotNull TransformMode mode,               // SUMMARIZE rejected with 400
        @NotBlank @Size(max = 2000) String instruction,
        @NotBlank @Size(max = 200_000) String markdown) {}
// Saved snapshot fields: aiAssisted (boolean), aiInstruction (text, null if not AI), aiMode (CLEAN_UP|CUSTOM)
```
SSE payloads are `token{text}`, `done{finishReason, truncated}` and `error{outcome}`; `outcome` is an enum (OFFLINE, BUSY, MODEL_MISSING, TOO_LARGE, DEADLINE, FAILED) so the UI never parses prose. rag-service events are parsed with `@JsonIgnoreProperties(ignoreUnknown = true)` records. No retry on generation: a retry repeats a long call on one GPU and the user can click again. A malformed event gives `FAILED`, logging only the event name. `FactDiff` is a pure function using regexes for ticket keys (`[A-Z][A-Z0-9]+-\d+`) and version strings, returning missing and added sets; it warns and does not gate (D-14-11).

### Async-First Design

Servlet async, not reactive: `SseEmitter` plus a virtual thread with blocking `RestClient` reads. The common mistake is running the transform on the request thread or inside `@Transactional`, which holds a container thread and a DB connection for up to 120 s. Resolve the document in a short transaction first, then stream with none. Stream tokens for UX; the key diff, length check and Save enablement run only after `done`. `ThreadLocal` context does not follow to the virtual thread, so pass everything explicitly.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- The instruction comes from config (or the user for CUSTOM). The document goes only in the `text` field and rag-service fences it as data. The main app builds no prompt around the document.
- A custom instruction is just another instruction: same 2000-char cap, outside the fence, trusted no more than the user.
- No ticket or commit context is sent (D-14-08).
- Each per-mode/per-type instruction must say to keep the Markdown structure; keep keys, versions, dates, environments and step order; add no facts; and return only the document.
- No few-shot: a 7B model at 4096 cannot afford examples. Tune the wording against the golden transform fixtures.
- `num_predict` and `num_ctx` are always set by rag-service. The main app enforces instruction, document and edit-buffer caps.
- Persist the instruction with the revision (ASST-10); log mode and document type only, never text.

### Context Window Management

No retrieval, history or tool loop. The strategy is the pre-flight size check from Section 4. Never truncate, and never summarise-then-clean, because either drops facts silently. After the call, `done.truncated` or `finishReason=length` means the result is partial: show a warning and block Save. Alert operationally if `promptTokens / num_ctx` exceeds 0.95.

### Cost and Latency Budget

- No per-token money cost (local model); the budget is GPU seconds.
- At the Phase 10 measured 1.3 to 7.7 tokens/s (warm TTFT 0.45 to 2.9 s, first call after load up to 15 s), a 5,000-character document (about 1,700 output tokens) takes roughly 4 to 20+ minutes. That is likely over the 120 s deadline and is the main latency risk (my arithmetic from those numbers, not a measurement). The live plan must measure a near-limit document and lower the limit or raise the deadline accordingly.
- One in-flight call per client (429 with `Retry-After`). Disable the buttons while streaming and show BUSY on 429.
- No caching: documents change, and a stale result adds risk with no measured benefit.
- No model routing: there are no sub-tasks, and a second model would compete for the 4 GB GPU.
- The cheapest call is the pre-flight rejection of a too-long document, which never reaches the model.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Two tiers, following Phase 10 and Phase 13, because CI has no GPU and the application must never need Ollama to build:

- **Hermetic tier (`mvn test`, `npm test`; runs everywhere, model-free).** A fake `RagTransformPort` (and WireMock for the adapter's SSE parsing) drives `AssistantTransformService`, `AiRevisionService`, `FactDiff` and the viewer UI. It proves everything the code owns: gating, saving, warnings, blocks, logs.
- **Live tier (opt-in, needs rag-service plus Ollama).** A golden fixture of synthetic release documents runs through the real `RagTransformAdapter` and rag-service `/v1/transform`, using the real `app.assistant.prompts.*` text. It measures what the model does with those prompts and how long it takes.

The live tier is where the model-dependent failure modes (1, 2 and 4 in Section 1) are measured. Hermetic tests cannot say anything about them.

### Coverage beyond D-14-11

D-14-11 stays as decided: the ticket-key and version diff warns and never blocks, and its scope does not change in this phase. The diff cannot see a key attached to the wrong item, an environment change or a date change (Section 1b). The eval handles that gap this way, without product scope:

1. **Measure it.** D2 and D3 to D5 run on every Live result. The report adds one number, the **diff-blind failure rate**: the share of Clean up case-runs where any of D2 to D5 fails but `FactDiff` (the same code the UI uses, called from the harness) reports no missing or added key or version. That is the share of bad results the user would be shown no warning for.
2. **Pin the blind spot.** A hermetic `FactDiff` test asserts the known limits (a key moved to another item produces no warning; an environment swap produces no warning). If anyone later extends the diff, that test changes on purpose.
3. **Decision rule, recorded not enacted.** If the diff-blind failure rate on the production-host run is above 10% of Clean up case-runs, record "extend the diff to environments, dates and key-to-item binding" as a v1.1 scope question for the user. If it is at or below 10%, the existing banner and side-by-side review are judged sufficient. The 10% is a starting value for the user to confirm at the checkpoint, not a derived number. Either way D-14-11 stays warn-only.

### Latency decision rule

Measured dev speeds (Phase 10: 1.3 to 7.7 tokens/s, first call after load up to 15 s) against the 120 s deadline allow roughly 156 to 924 output tokens, about 470 to 2,770 characters of Clean up output (my arithmetic: tokens/s x 120 x 3.0 chars/token, ignoring load time). The estimator limit is about 5,300 characters (Section 4). So on the dev GPU a document well under the size limit is expected to hit `DEADLINE`. The rule below decides what to change, using measurements, on the host that will ship.

Inputs, measured by the Live run at three sizes (about 25%, 50% and 95% of the advertised limit; cases B01, B04 or A03, and N01/N02), 3 runs each:
- `r10`: 10th-percentile generation speed in tokens/s, computed as `completionTokens / (total - TTFT)`.
- `ttft95`: 95th-percentile time to first token, including a cold load (run once after `ollama stop`).
- `sizeLimit`: characters from the config-backed estimator at the production `num-ctx`.
- `D`: the current stream deadline (120 s).

Computation, with output length assumed equal to input length (Clean up) and 3.0 chars/token as in the estimator:
```
latencyLimitChars = floor( r10 * (D - ttft95) * 3.0 * 0.9 )     # 0.9 = safety margin
neededDeadline    = ttft95 + (sizeLimit / 3.0) / (r10 * 0.9)
```
1. `latencyLimitChars >= sizeLimit`: change nothing.
2. Otherwise, if `neededDeadline <= 300 s`: **raise the deadline** to `ceil(neededDeadline)` in rag-service and in `app.assistant.transform.stream-deadline` / `read-timeout` (keep the app values above rag-service's). The 300 s ceiling is my assumption for what a person will watch a stream for; the user confirms or changes it at the checkpoint. The cost is that the single in-flight slot blocks chat for that long (chat gets 429), so record it.
3. Otherwise **lower the size limit** to `latencyLimitChars` through a config cap (`app.assistant.transform.max-chars`, applied as `min(estimator, cap)` in the limits endpoint and the pre-stream check). Do not lower the app's `num-ctx` mirror for this purpose: it must stay equal to rag-service's.
4. Never both silently. After the change, re-run N01 and N02; they must finish `stop` 3/3. The chosen branch, inputs and resulting numbers go into the report.

On the dev host the numbers are recorded and the rule is evaluated for information; the binding evaluation is the production-host run (below).

Follows D-13-16 (split gate) and 10-09 (T03 accepted for dev only).

### Eval Tooling

**Primary Tool:** JUnit 5 plus the existing WireMock and Testcontainers setup for the hermetic tier, Vitest for the viewer tests, and a deterministic Java golden harness (extending the Phase 10 and Phase 13 harness) for the live tier. No new dependency, no Python, no external service.

Phoenix, RAGAS and Promptfoo defaults are overridden. Reason: RAGAS measures retrieval and wants an LLM judge, and there is no retrieval here. Phoenix and any tracing platform would capture document text, which SAFE-03 forbids (see Section 7). Promptfoo would add a Node harness beside a Java one for checks that are exact string and set comparisons. An LLM judge is not built: a 7B model judging a 7B model on "was the risk softened" is weaker than the deterministic checks plus a human read. If one is ever added it must be the local model, calibrated to at least 0.7 agreement with the human 0-2 scores, and never gating.

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Arize Phoenix default overridden, as in Phase 10 (SAFE-03). Reason: a tracing platform stores prompts and completions, and here those are whole release documents, instructions and rewritten results. Section 1 failure mode 5 forbids that, the model is local so there is nothing to send out, and the project rule against over-engineering applies to a single-user internal tool. Instead: one structured, metadata-only log line per transform and per save, plus Micrometer metrics through the existing Actuator, tagged by outcome and document type only, never by text. No document, instruction or result text is logged, traced or put in a metric tag; this includes exception messages and the instruction's first characters. Revisit only if a debugging need appears that the metadata cannot meet, and then only a self-hosted, content-free option.

**Key Metrics to Track:**

Log line per transform (no content): `requestId`, `releaseId`, `documentType`, `mode`, `instructionSource` (`CONFIG` or `CUSTOM`), `instructionChars`, `documentChars`, `limitChars`, `outcome` (`OK`, `TRUNCATED`, `TOO_LARGE_PREFLIGHT`, `TOO_LARGE_SERVER`, `BUSY`, `OFFLINE`, `MODEL_MISSING`, `DEADLINE`, `FAILED`, `CLIENT_GONE`), `finishReason`, `promptTokens`, `completionTokens`, `promptTokens/numCtx`, `ttftMs`, `totalMs`, `tokensPerSecond`. The rag-service log line already carries `PROMPT_VERSION` and the model tag; correlate by `requestId` or time, and add a hash of the six prompt texts to the app's startup log.

Log line per save (no content): `requestId`, `releaseId`, `documentType`, `mode`, `sourceRevision`, new revision number, `editedChars`, and `missingKeys`, `addedKeys`, `missingVersions`, `addedVersions` as counts only, recomputed server-side from the source revision and the saved Markdown with the same `FactDiff` (logging only, no change to the D-14-11 behaviour). A rejected `SUMMARIZE` save logs a `SUMMARIZE_REJECTED` outcome.

Metrics:
1. Outcome counts by mode and document type, including `DEADLINE`, `TRUNCATED`, `BUSY`, both `TOO_LARGE` kinds.
2. Share of runs that complete (`OK`) per document type, and the share of `OK` runs that are later saved.
3. Saved AI revisions per document type, and the share saved with a non-zero key/version diff (saved after a warning).
4. Context ratio (`promptTokens / numCtx`) and `finishReason=length` count.
5. TTFT, total time and tokens/s (the inputs to the latency rule), by document-size bucket (below 25%, 25 to 60%, above 60% of the limit).

**Alert Thresholds:**

There is no pager. "Alert" means a visible Actuator-backed check or a scheduled log review. Starting values for a one-user tool; revise after the first live baseline.

| Signal | Threshold |
|--------|-----------|
| Context ratio | Any request above 0.95 (probable truncation; also an estimator-drift signal) |
| Estimator drift | Any `TOO_LARGE_SERVER` where the pre-flight said the document fits (the app and rag-service formulas disagree) |
| Deadline | More than 2% of runs end `DEADLINE` on the production host, or any run on a document below 60% of the limit |
| Truncation | More than 5% of completed runs have `finishReason=length` (the limit or `num_predict` is wrong for this model) |
| Unsafe save | Any saved runbook or approval revision whose run ended `length`, `truncated` or `DEADLINE` (UI Save block failed, treat as a defect) |
| Summary save | Not an alert: SUMMARIZE saves are allowed (D-14-13) |
| Warned saves | More than 30% of saved AI revisions carry a non-zero key/version diff (starting value; check whether the model or the prompt regressed) |
| Slot leak | In-flight gauge above zero with no request for 5 minutes after a browser abort |
| Readiness | `OFFLINE` or `MODEL_MISSING` persisting |
| Log content | Any canary or document text found by the periodic log-hygiene check: treat as a SAFE-03 incident |

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

Content is not stored in logs, so sampling is by metadata signal and the review is a read of the saved revision against its source (saved AI revisions are application data, not logs), or a re-run on synthetic text for anything that was discarded.
- Review the first 20 saved runbook and approval AI revisions in full, then every saved revision with any of: non-zero key/version diff counts, `documentChars / limitChars` above 0.8, `mode=CUSTOM` on a runbook or approval, or `finishReason=length`. Release notes: 10% sample.
- Weight attention to runs with `DEADLINE`, `TRUNCATED` or a context ratio above 0.9: they are the near-limit documents where the rollback section is the part most likely to be cut.
- A user who sees a bad result reports the request id from the UI. The engineer matches it to the metadata line, reproduces with a synthetic document and adds a fixture case (Section 6 flywheel). The viewer already shows the AI-assisted badge, banner and recorded instruction, which is the audit trail for saved revisions.
